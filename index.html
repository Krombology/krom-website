How an engagement runs

The same five steps whether it is a 50-seat migration or a three-company integration. Each step ends with something written that you keep. You always know what happens next.

  1. 1

    Assess

    One to two weeks. Inventory of identity, mail, files, servers, and devices. Who shares what with whom. What breaks if it moves.

    You keep: inventory workbook, risk list, a scope you can price.

  2. 2

    Plan

    A written integration path with scope, risk, and phasing per workstream. Communication templates for your people. A rollback for every cut.

    You keep: the plan, the schedule, the notices your staff will receive.

  3. 3

    Execute

    Batches during business hours, cutovers at quiet ones. A status note every day the project is live. Nothing bulk runs without a dry run first.

    You keep: daily status, a log of every change made.

  4. 4

    Stabilize

    Delta checks against the source. Fix what the migration tool missed. Tune spam filtering and security. Train the staff.

    You keep: per-user verification report, tuned policies, training notes.

  5. 5

    Hand off

    Documentation and runbooks your team can run without me. Old licences cancelled. Or Krom stays on as your IT.

    You keep: runbooks in your wiki, credentials in your vault, the savings.

Questions people ask first

If yours is not here, the contact form takes it.

We have an IT team. Why bring in Krom?

Because your team has a day job, and a migration is a second one. Krom does the specialist part, works alongside your people, and leaves them the runbook. The best outcome is your team running the environment better than before, without needing me.

We have no IT team at all. Is that a problem?

No, it is the second thing Krom is for. Projects for companies without IT come with more hand-holding on the front end, and most of them turn into a retainer afterwards so there is someone to call.

What size company is this for?

Fifty to about a thousand people is the sweet spot. Under fifty, a retainer usually makes more sense than a project. Over a thousand, Krom is one engineer, so the work is scoped to a workstream rather than the whole programme.

Do you work remotely or on site?

Remote first. On site across New York and New Jersey when the job calls for it, which for server rooms and acquisitions it often does.

What happens when the migration tool says it succeeded but something is missing?

That is the part Krom is known for. Migration tools report success per item they saw. They do not report items they never saw. Every Krom migration includes a separate comparison of source and destination, written independently of the tool, and the gaps get fixed before anything is switched off.

Can you cut our licence bill without breaking anything?

Yes, and the order matters. Usage is measured from the vendor's own logs, removals go out in waves with a personal notice and a date, and anyone who objects is held back. The subscription itself is downgraded last, once everyone who needs a seat has one.

Will you resell us licences or hardware?

No. Krom is paid for engineering time only. Recommendations are not shaped by a margin on what you buy.

How fast can you start?

A 30 minute call this week, a written assessment proposal within two business days of it, and assessment work usually starting within two weeks.

What do you need from us to begin?

An admin account with the right roles, a person on your side who can answer questions, and a list of the dates that matter, such as audits, renewals, and peak season. Everything else Krom will inventory.
